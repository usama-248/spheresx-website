Product operations

Planning maintenance before your app goes live

Practical guidance for founders and teams planning their next digital product.

In this articleGive the live product an ownerSeparate urgent problems from improvementsBudget for the services behind the appKeep a manageable improvement cycle

SpheresX Journal · 1 min read

Give the live product an owner

Launching an application is the beginning of operating it. Someone needs to review user reports, make product decisions and coordinate technical work. That person does not need to fix every issue, but they should know how a problem is reported and who is responsible for the next action. Establishing ownership early makes support more predictable for both the business and the development team.

Separate urgent problems from improvements

A sign-in failure affecting users needs a different response from a request for a new dashboard filter. Define a small set of issue priorities based on user impact and agree how they are communicated. Keep improvements in a separate backlog so that they can be planned deliberately. Include useful details in reports: the action attempted, the result, the device or environment and steps that reproduce the problem. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information in screenshots or logs.

Budget for the services behind the app

Hosting, databases, messaging, maps, AI services and other integrations may have ongoing costs. Record which services are used and who monitors their usage. Review whether a change in customer activity could change those costs. Maintenance discussions should also cover access ownership, dependency updates and release preparation. The right level of support depends on the application and the consequences of downtime; one standard package will not fit every product.

Keep a manageable improvement cycle

Collect feedback and review it on an agreed schedule. Group related issues, identify the journeys causing the most friction and choose a small set of improvements for the next release. Check the affected user journeys before shipping and keep release notes that explain meaningful changes. Consistent, focused improvements help a product remain understandable to its users and maintainable for the people building it.
